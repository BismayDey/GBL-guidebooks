About this game

WireUp teaches Electrical Installation by putting you on a working site as an apprentice. You walk around, talk to the people who work there, and carry out the real tasks of the trade at real workstations. There are no multiple-choice questions anywhere: you are assessed on the work you actually do.

How to play — the complete guide

WireUp is a living world, not a quiz. Nobody asks you multiple-choice questions. You walk to a real workstation, open it, and do the job — and the objective ticks the moment the work is actually correct. This section covers every control and every part of the screen. The two demos below are live: try them.

1. Try the joystick

This is the real control, running here in the page. Press anywhere in the dark panel and drag. Notice that wherever you press becomes the centre, that any direction works, and that how far you drag changes the speed.

The dot moves at the speed your drag is asking for.

  • Press anywhere on the left halfYou never have to find a fixed stick or look down — wherever your thumb lands becomes the centre.
  • Any direction, not just fourFull 360-degree movement. Drag diagonally and you walk diagonally.
  • Drag distance sets speedA small drag walks slowly — ideal for lining up with a bench. Dragging to the edge runs. Dragging further does not go faster; it is capped.
  • Let go to stopThe stick springs back to centre and the character stops immediately.

2. Try the direction pad

If you prefer buttons, switch to the pad. Press the buttons below — then deliberately press the diagonal gap between two of them.

What registers

Press a button, then try the diagonal gap between two of them.

Each button only responds inside its own circle. That is why pressing near the edge of one can never trigger its neighbour — the gap simply does nothing, instead of sending you the wrong way.

  • Press and hold to walkHold a button to keep walking that way; release to stop. Each button shows the direction it moves you.
  • Switch between joystick and pad any timeTap the small switch button beside the controls. It is labelled with what you will switch to, and your choice is remembered.

3. Before you start

  • Hold the phone sideways (landscape)The world is a wide view. In portrait you get a "rotate your device" screen; turn the phone and it starts immediately.
  • Go fullscreenTap the expand button in the corner. On a phone this hides the address bar and makes every control noticeably bigger.
  • Sound is on by defaultThere is an SFX toggle in the top bar. A chime confirms each objective, but the game is fully playable silent.

4. What is on the screen

  • Objectives panel — top leftYour current unit and its tasks. The task you are on has an arrow; finished tasks show a tick. Tap the header to collapse it if it covers the view; it remembers your choice.
  • Status bar — across the topDay and clock, the unit dots showing how far you have come, and your running Standard percentage.
  • Action button — bottom rightThe round E button: how you use whatever you are standing next to.
  • Site Plan button — top rightOpens the full map of the site.

5. On a computer

  • KeyboardW A S D or the arrow keys to walk, E to use, Space to advance dialogue, Esc to close, M for the map.
  • Click to walk thereClick a spot and the character walks to it. Click a workstation and they walk over and open it.

6. Doing the work

  • Walk close, then press E or the round buttonWhen you are near something usable, a label appears over it, e.g. "[E] Sorting bench". That label means the action button will work.
  • No label means you are not close enoughWalk nearer until it shows. Pressing with nothing nearby does nothing — you have not broken anything.
  • People work the same wayStand next to a colleague and press the same button to talk. They give you the unit's instructions.

7. Being shown where to go

You should never wander wondering what to do. Three cues point at your next task and all update the instant you finish one.

  • The "!" markerFloats over the building holding your next task; once inside it moves onto the exact bench or desk. It hides when you are close enough for that station's own label to appear — at which point just press the action button.
  • The heading arrowAlways points toward your target, so you know which way to set off.
  • The edge arrowIf the target is off screen, the arrow pins to the screen edge in that direction.

8. The Site Plan (the map)

  • Open it any timeTap Site Plan top right, or press M. It fills the screen.
  • What it showsEvery building and the paths between them, to scale. A coloured dot is you; a ring marks the building you need next.
  • Close itTap the X, tap outside the panel, press Esc, or press M again. You do not move while it is open.

9. Task panels — where the work happens

  • Sliders and dialsWhere you set a real value — a temperature, a depth, a mix consistency — drag the slider roughly into place, then use − and + to fine-tune exactly.
  • Choice cards and checklistsTap a card to select, or tick items in order. Some tasks require the correct sequence, not just the correct items.
  • Confirm buttonsNothing is recorded until you press the confirm button, so you can change your mind freely.
  • Closing a panelTap the X or press Esc. Anything already completed stays completed.
Objectives tick straight away. The moment your work is correct the objective ticks and you hear a chime — while you are still at the bench. You do not have to leave the building for it to register.

10. Conversations, finishing, and saving

  • Advance a lineTap the dialogue box, or press E or Space. Press Esc to leave early — instructions stay in the objectives panel.
  • Finishing a levelA congratulations screen shows units mastered, your Standard percentage and the certificate level reached. The marker then points at the certification board.
  • Saving is automaticThere is no save button; progress reloads when you return. It is stored in that browser on that device, so a different phone or clearing browsing data starts fresh. Each level saves separately.

11. If something goes wrong

How you are assessed

  • By your actions, not your answersEvery objective is a check against what you have actually done — the right PPE on, the mix at the right consistency, the delivery sorted correctly. Nothing to memorise, nothing to guess.
  • The Standard percentageThe figure in the top bar rises as you demonstrate competence. It records the quality of what you did, not just that you finished.
  • Your certificate levelThe completion screen shows the standard you reached, and the certification board in the office records it.
  • You can retryNothing is lost by getting something wrong. Reopen the station and do it properly — the objective ticks when the work is right.
